The Digital Divide: Internet Access & Freedom

Exploring how connectivity and online freedom vary by country

Why this dashboard?

This dashboard explores the relationship between two ideas that are often conflated but are not the same: internet access (how many people are online) and internet freedom (how free those people are to access, publish and organize online). Using multiple views global choropleths with zoom and year sliders, a map of the first year each country passed the 50% adoption threshold, a 2024 freedom score map, a bubble chart of access vs. freedom (with outlier annotations), a 1990-2023 line view for the most connected countries, an overview + detail time series, a dumbbell chart comparing 2023 access vs. 2024 freedom, a simple status breakdown, and side-by-side bars for countries with both metrics-we surface patterns that are easy to miss in tables. Together, these views show that connectivity has expanded rapidly, but freedoms vary widely: Nordic countries cluster high on both metrics, while several high access Gulf and post Soviet states display large access and freedom gaps. Use the sliders, dropdowns, legends and tooltips to drill down into specific countries and years.

Global Internet Access (1990-Latest) - Quartiles

This choropleth maps the share of people online for any chosen year, grouped into quartiles: darker blue means higher access, lighter blue lower access for that year's distribution. Use the Year slider to watch adoption spread from early leaders in North America, Western Europe, Oceania and East Asia in the 1990s to broader uptake across Latin America, Eastern Europe and North Africa in the 2000s-2010s. The Zoom, Lon and Lat sliders let you focus on regions or small countries. You can hover over a country for exact values. The view highlights strong global progress, but also persistent pockets of low access in parts of Sub-Saharan Africa, illustrating an uneven digital divide.

Year Each Country First Reached >=50% Online

This map shows the first year each country's internet adoption crossed the 50% of population threshold. The blue colour scale encodes the year (darker = more recent) whereas countries in orange have not yet reached 50%. Hover for exact dates. You can quickly spot early adopters (Nordics, North America, Western Europe, Oceania) versus later surges across Latin America and parts of Asia in the 2010s. Clustered orange in parts of Sub-Saharan Africa and a few Asian states highlights where connectivity remains below a majority today. You may notice outliers such in their continents such as North Korea and Papua New Guinea. For reasons unkonwn (perhaps it only becoming a country in 2011), South Sudan has no data.

Freedom Status Breakdown

This simple bar chart summarizes the 2024 Internet Freedom Status across countries: Free (F), Partly Free (PF) and Not Free (NF). Bars show the number of countries in each category, using a consistent blue palette where darker shades indicate stronger freedom. Hover any bar to see the exact count. Use this as a quick snapshot of the global landscape before diving into country-level detail in the other views.

Internet Freedom Score (2024)

This choropleth maps each country's 2024 Freedom on the Net score (0-100), shaded from light (more restrictions) to dark blue (greater online freedom). Use the Zoom slider to inspect regions in detail and the Lon/Lat sliders to pan the map's center. You'll notice consistently high scores across much of Western Europe, North America and Oceania, while parts of the Middle East, Africa and Central Asia show lighter tones, indicating tighter controls on access, content and user rights. Hover any country to compare scores and spot regional contrasts at a glance.

Access vs Freedom (Latest)

This bubble chart compares each country's % of population online (x-axis) with its Freedom on the Net score (y-axis, 0-100). Bubbles are shaded on a single blue scale (darker means higher freedom) and sized by the share of people online, so larger, darker bubbles indicate both high access and strong digital rights. The dotted guides at 60% online and 70/100 freedom divide the space into informative quadrants. For example, the upper-right shows places with both high connectivity and freedom. Hover to see exact values; notable points such as Iceland (94% online, 94/100 freedom) are annotated to illustrate the upper bound. Use the legend to interpret status colours and the bubble size key to relate area to access.

Connectivity Progress: 2000 → 2023

This slope-dumbbell chart compares each country's internet access in 2000 (left diamond) to 2023 (right circle). Read each row left to right: the longer the arrow, the bigger the gain in % of the population online. Most countries show large jumps with many moving from single digits in 2000 to 80-95%+ by 2023, while a few still lag behind. Point colour encodes the latest Freedom on the Net status (F/PF/NF), highlighting that rapid connectivity growth does not always coincide with high online freedom. Hover any row to see exact values and scan arrow lengths to spot the biggest improvers, and compare rows with the same colour to contrast progress among similarly rated countries.

Country Snapshot: Access vs Freedom (Dumbbell)

This dumbbell plot compares each country's internet access in 2023 (left round marker) with its Freedom on the Net score in 2024 (right square). Countries are the top 30 by access and are listed down the y-axis while the x-axis is a 0-100 scale. The connecting line shows the 'gap' between being online and being free online where shorter lines indicate that access and freedom are similar, while long lines reveal places where connectivity far outpaces civil liberties. Square colour encodes the latest freedom status (F/PF/NF). Use the “Filter by status” dropdown to focus on one status and hover points to see exact values. Note, for example, the annotation for Belarus: despite very high access (91.5%), its freedom score is very low (22), illustrating how availability does not guarantee openness.

Top 10 Most Connected Countries (1990-2023)

This line chart tracks the top 10 most connected countries from 1990-2023, showing the share of each country's population online (y-axis) over time (x-axis). Use the legend on the right to identify each line and hover anywhere to see exact year-value pairs. Two broad patterns emerge: (1) early adopters such as Iceland, Norway and Luxembourg climb steadily from the late 1990s and approach saturation (95-100%) by the mid-2010s. (2) several Gulf states-including Qatar, United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Bahrain, show rapid growth in the 2000s, catching up to the early leaders by the 2010s. Despite different starting points and growth rates, by the early 2020s all ten countries converge near universal access, highlighting how quickly late adopters closed the gap once infrastructure and mobile broadband scaled.

Internet Access - Overview + Detail

This overview + detail view shows a single country's internet adoption from 1990-2023. The top line tracks the share of the population online each year while the bottom area chart provides context and lets you focus on a time window. Use the Country dropdown to switch nations, then drag across the bottom chart to brush a period. The top chart zooms to that selection so you can inspect inflection points (e.g. broadband and smartphone take-off years). Hover any point for exact values. This layout makes it easy to compare a country's early slow growth to later acceleration and see when it approached saturation.

Access vs Freedom - Side-by-Side Bars

This side-by-side bar chart compares Internet access in 2023 (blue) with the Internet freedom score in 2024 (green) for countries where both metrics are available. Read each pair left to right: tall blue with similarly tall green bars (e.g. Iceland, Estonia, Canada) indicate places that are both highly connected and relatively free online. In contrast, countries such as the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Kazakhstan, Saudi Arabia and Belarus show very high access but much lower freedom scores-evidence of a strong connectivity-freedom gap. Hover a bar to see exact values and use the x-axis labels to scan regional patterns.

What did we learn?

Three takeaways stand out. First, the world crossed a series of adoption tipping points after 2000 where most countries reached >=50% online only in the past decade, which the “Year reached >=50%” map makes clear. Second, more access does not guarantee more freedom: the dumbbell and side-by-side bar charts highlight countries with near-universal connectivity but low freedom scores (e.g. Bahrain, Kazakhstan, Belarus). Third, a subset of countries sustain both high access and high freedom-Nordics, parts of Western Europe, and a few Asia-Pacific economies-showing the combination is possible but policy-dependent. These visuals are a snapshot (freedom is shown for 2024), so trends may shift; nonetheless, the overall picture is of a two-speed digital world where infrastructure has outpaced rights in many places. Use this dashboard to spot peers, identify outliers, and frame questions for deeper research or policy design.

Notes & limitations: Freedom scores come from a single recent year, while access is a long series. The differences in methodology across sources and missing data for some territories may affect comparisons. Choropleths emphasize geography over population size.
